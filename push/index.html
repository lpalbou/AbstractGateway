    Push to the watch and the iPhone¶

    When something needs you — an approval, a question, a published alert, an automation's result or failure — the AbstractAssistant apps on your Apple Watch and iPhone show it within seconds, even when the app is closed. This page explains how that works, the one setting a self-hoster may change, how to run your own relay with your own Apple key, and exactly what leaves your gateway.

    It builds on your event stream: a push is only a hint that the stream holds something new; the app reads the real event from your gateway. For the other notification paths (the desktop tray, email) see tray.md and email.md.

    For people: nothing to configure¶

    Sign in to the AbstractAssistant app on your watch or iPhone and allow notifications. The app registers itself with your gateway at sign-in and refreshes the registration at least once a week; signing out removes it. The system's notification permission for the app is the only switch.

    You can see the devices that get your alerts in the console: My email → Notifications → Devices (web and terminal console) lists each watch or iPhone with its name, when it last registered, the last alert sent, and the last error in plain words.

    What you get:

    Something waits for you Alert Level
    a run asks for your approval Approval needed time-sensitive
    a run asks you a question Question time-sensitive
    a flow published an alert to you the publication's title active (banner and sound)
    an automation has a result has a result active
    an automation failed failed active

    An approval that is answered before the push leaves (on another device, in the console) is never pushed. A watch registered in the last 24 hours takes the alerts; otherwise the iPhone does, so your wrist rings once. A registration not refreshed for 7 days expires.

    A flow's Notify me alert can choose where it goes (its channels: tray, watch, phone, email, console). Push then rings only the listed apps, the phone first among them (the iPhone app and the watch app are one family, so Apple mirrors the phone's alert to the watch; a watch signed in on its own is pushed directly): an alert for watch alone never falls back to the iPhone, and one that lists neither is not pushed. An alert with no channels goes everywhere, as above. GET /api/gateway/runs/{run_id}/after shows, for a run, which alerts it raised and where each was delivered (api.md).

    For the operator: nothing to set¶

    The gateway sends its push requests to a push forwarder (the relay described below). The address is built in: the publisher's forwarder, once it is live. This release has none built in, so pushes stay off until you run your own forwarder.

    Your own forwarder¶

    One setting, nothing else. As an admin, open My email → Notifications → Push forwarder in the web or terminal console, choose Edit, then Your own forwarder, and give its address: https://…, or http:// for a forwarder on the same computer (http://127.0.0.1:8095). The other two choices are Built-in forwarder (the default) and Off. Save asks the forwarder for its apps once (GET <address>/v1/info) and says what it answered or why it could not be reached; the address is saved either way and used at once, without a restart. The setting is gateway-wide, in <gateway data dir>/push/settings.json (nothing secret in it); the API is GET|PUT /api/gateway/push/settings.

    A launch flag overrides the saved setting for one run:

    abstractgateway serve --push-relay https://push.example.org   # or --push-relay off

    The address in use is the --push-relay flag of this run, else the saved setting, else the built-in forwarder. While the flag wins, the console says set by the launch flag for this run. There is no environment variable.

    • With no address (off), the gateway runs no push dispatcher: no thread, no outbound connection. Saving an address starts it.
    • With an address but no registered device, the dispatcher does no work and opens no connection. The gateway contacts the forwarder only once a device has registered.
    • Turning the forwarder on never pushes what happened while it was off.
    • In the split layout (serve --no-runner plus abstractgateway runner), the runner process sends and reads the saved setting too: without a flag it keeps one parked thread that checks the settings file every 2 seconds, so a saved address reaches it without a restart. A --push-relay flag goes to both processes.

    Delivery is retried for an hour (after 2 s, 10 s, 60 s, 5 min, then every 15 min). An approval or a question that could not be delivered in that hour is recorded as notification.failed in your event stream; each delivered alert is recorded as notification.sent with channel watch or phone and via: "push". A relay outage writes at most one log line per hour.

    State lives in the data folder of each account (<data dir>/push/, private files): devices.json (the registrations), cursor.json (the last event read; the first start begins at the newest event, so history is never pushed) and outbox.sqlite3 (one row per alert and device, at most 5,000, finished rows kept 7 days). <gateway data dir>/push/token_owners.json records which account holds each device token: when another account signs in on the same app, the token moves to it.

    Run your own relay¶

    Apple only delivers notifications signed with the app publisher's key, so a self-hosted gateway cannot talk to Apple directly. The relay is the small service that holds that key. It ships with the gateway:

    pip install "abstractgateway[push-relay]"
abstractgateway push-relay \
  --apns-key-file AuthKey_ABC123DEFG.p8 --key-id ABC123DEFG --team-id TEAM123456 \
  --topic com.example.assistant --topic com.example.assistant.watchkitapp \
  --listen 127.0.0.1:8095
    • --apns-key-file, --key-id, --team-id: the APNs authentication key (.p8) from your Apple developer account, its Key ID and your Team ID.
    • --topic: the bundle ids of the apps you build and sign; any other topic is refused.
    • --listen (default 127.0.0.1:8095): put a TLS reverse proxy in front of it, and pass --forwarded-allow-ips with the proxy's address so the per-IP limit sees the caller.

    The push-relay extra adds the HTTP/2 client Apple requires (httpx[http2]) and cryptography for the provider token. A gateway that only sends to a relay needs neither.

    Then save its address as the gateway's Push forwarder (Your own forwarder). A gateway accepts registrations for the two packaged apps (ai.abstractframework.assistant, the iPhone app, and ai.abstractframework.assistant.watchkitapp, its watch companion) and for the topics your relay lists at GET /v1/info.

    The relay is stateless: it stores nothing, keeps its rate limits in memory and reuses its signed provider token for at most 40 minutes. Limits: 30 alerts an hour and 300 a day per device token, 600 requests an hour per source address.

    Privacy: what the relay and Apple see¶

    The relay accepts no text. A request is exactly:

    {"topic": "ai.abstractframework.assistant.watchkitapp", "env": "production",
 "token": "<APNs device token>", "kind": "approval",
 "collapse": "<32 hex>", "seq": 42, "reg": "<16-32 hex>"}
    • kind is one of approval, question, notify, result, failure.
    • collapse is sha256("attention:" + record_key) cut to 32 hex characters: an opaque id that lets the system replace a notification instead of showing it twice. Run ids and record keys never leave the gateway.
    • seq is the event's number in your stream, so the app can fetch exactly that event.
    • reg is a handle the app chose at sign-in; an alert meant for an earlier sign-in shows as From an earlier sign-in.

    The relay and Apple therefore see the device token, the app, the kind of alert, the event number and the time — never a title, a name, a prompt or any content. The relay's logs hold sha256(token) cut to 12 characters, never a token. The relay also sees your gateway's IP address, and only once a device has registered.

    The fixed messages¶

    The relay builds the notification itself, always the same localized message per kind (title-loc-key: push.<kind>.title, loc-key: push.<kind>.body), so a device token in the wrong hands can never carry words under the app's name. The app's notification service extension then fetches the event from your gateway and replaces the generic text with the real one (the name of the automation, the title of the publication). If the fetch times out, the generic text stays.

    The APNs request: apns-push-type: alert, apns-priority 10 for approvals and questions and 5 otherwise, apns-collapse-id = collapse, expiry after 24 hours, and the body {aps: {alert: {title-loc-key, loc-key}, sound: "default", mutable-content: 1, interruption-level: "time-sensitive" | "active", thread-id: <kind>}, s: <seq>, r: <reg>}.
